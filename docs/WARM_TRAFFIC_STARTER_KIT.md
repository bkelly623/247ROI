## Positioning

The message:

> The first automation should be the expensive bottleneck, not just the task everyone finds annoying.

## LinkedIn Post Drafts

### Post 1

Do not automate the annoying task first.

Automate the expensive bottleneck first.

For most owner-led businesses, the best first AI or automation project is usually close to one of these:

- slow lead response
- stale follow-up
- manual reporting
- inbox triage
- CRM updates
- estimate prep
- scattered handoffs
- owner status chasing

The filter is simple:

Does it happen often?
Is it close to money, labor, customer delay, or owner attention?
Are the inputs and outputs clear?
Can humans still approve sensitive decisions?

I put together a practical checklist here:
https://www.get247roi.com/what-should-my-business-automate-first

### Post 2

A lot of small businesses do not need an "AI strategy."

They need one workflow to stop leaking time.

That might be:

- leads getting answered faster
- estimates getting followed up
- reports building themselves
- inboxes getting triaged
- bid invites getting organized
- dashboards showing what needs attention

The first build should be narrow enough to prove.

One workflow.
One useful output.
One success metric.

I wrote the checklist I use to think through it:
https://www.get247roi.com/what-should-my-business-automate-first

### Post 3

If you are asking "what should my business automate first?", do not start with the tool.

Start with the bottleneck.

AI is useful when the work needs reading, summarizing, drafting, classifying, research, or flexible handoff prep.

Regular automation is better for predictable data movement, reminders, routing, and record updates.

Dashboards are better when the owner cannot see what is happening.

Internal apps are better when the team needs queues, approvals, records, and workflow control.
